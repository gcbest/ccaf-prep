Section 8, as a graph

The Claude Agent SDK broken into 44 micro-topics wired by prerequisite, taught one knowledge point at a time, and kept alive by spaced review that pays credit down the graph. Built the Math Academy way.

How this works

This page is a small implementation of the system described in The Math Academy Way, applied to one exam section instead of to mathematics. Six ideas do all the work.

1 · The knowledge graph

Section 8 is cut into 44 micro-topics, each small enough to teach and assess on its own, and wired together by prerequisite edges. A hard edge gates: you do not see a topic until everything behind it is mastered. A soft edge only orders the queue. The graph — not the reading order of the notes — is the source of truth about what comes next.

2 · The knowledge frontier

The boundary between what you know and what you are ready to learn. Every new lesson you are served sits exactly on it. The Graph tab draws it: filled nodes are mastered, outlined nodes are ready, faded nodes are still locked behind something.

3 · Scaffolded mastery learning

Each topic is a short lesson broken into knowledge points — the first covers the most basic idea, later ones introduce the harder cases. You clear a knowledge point by answering its questions correctly, and only then does the next one open. Advanced topics unlock when the topic as a whole is mastered, not when you have merely read it.

4 · Targeted remediation

Fail the same knowledge point twice and the lesson stops. Rather than lowering the bar, the system queues a remedial review on that knowledge point's key prerequisites — the specific earlier topics it leans on — so you clear the bar fully on the next attempt.

5 · Encompassings and fractional implicit repetition

Advanced topics implicitly practise simpler ones. Reasoning about what comes back out of a subagent exercises context isolation whether you meant to or not. Those relationships are recorded as encompassing edges with a coverage weight, and reviewing an advanced topic pays discounted repetition credit down to everything it encompasses. Credit that arrives too early counts for less. When credit reaches a full repetition, the simpler topic's review schedule moves on without you ever seeing it in the queue.

The same edges drive review compression: when several due topics are covered by one advanced topic, you are served the advanced one and the rest are paid implicitly. That is why the review queue stays short as the graph fills up.

6 · Spaced repetition and XP

A cleared topic comes back after 1 day, then 3, 7, 16, 35, 75, 160 — each successful repetition pushing the next one further out. XP rewards both quantity and quality: full credit for clearing something first try, partial credit after a stumble, a bonus for mastering a topic outright.
